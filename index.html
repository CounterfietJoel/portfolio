Case Studies

How the Courses Were Designed

The decisions behind the build: who the learners were, what they needed to do, and why the design looks the way it does.

U21MI503

UAV Technology: a gamified web module

Learners
Undergraduate Mechatronics Engineering students taking UAV Technology.
My role
Course faculty, instructional designer, and developer.
Scope
UAV history, classification, and subsystems.

Design decisions

  • Assessment inside the content, not after it. Gamified checks sit at the end of each topic, so learners practice retrieval while the material is fresh.
  • Visible progress. Progress telemetry shows learners how far they have come and shows the instructor where a cohort is getting stuck.
  • Interactive web, not slides. A browser-based module runs on any campus machine or phone with no installs or LMS plugins.

Entrepreneurship

One startup story across a whole course

Learners
Engineering undergraduates in an Entrepreneurship Development & Startup course.
My role
Course faculty, instructional designer, and developer.
Scope
Every unit, from idea to venture.

Design decisions

  • A single running scenario. Learners follow one fictional company, Sensovate Technologies, through every unit, so each new concept lands on a story they already know.
  • Theory in context. Each unit's ideas are applied to Sensovate's next decision instead of a fresh, disconnected example.
  • Open on the web. The full course is published as a site anyone can browse.

Principles

Make the complex learnable.

  • Rigor before polish

    Understand the material properly before designing anything. I ask for the schematic, the data, the paper.

  • Clarity is respect

    Respecting an audience means not wasting its attention: one idea per slide, plain language, generous space.

  • Simple, not shallow

    Make it easy without dumbing it down. Real terminology gets explained, not removed.

Process

How I Work

The same five steps whether it's a full course or a single keynote.

  1. 01

    Discover

    Interview the subject expert, read the source material, and find out who the learners or the audience really are.

  2. 02

    Define outcomes

    Write what learners should be able to do afterward, in terms that can be checked.

  3. 03

    Storyboard

    Outline the flow and the assessment blueprint before any visuals, so review happens while changes are cheap.

  4. 04

    Build

    Produce the module or deck with the subject expert reviewing for accuracy at each milestone.

  5. 05

    Test & revise

    Run it with real learners, look at where they struggle, and fix those parts first.
